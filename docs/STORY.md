# God Game — Story Bible

*⚠️ Contains every spoiler. This file records the **true** story and **when** the player learns each part.*
*💡 = Claude's suggestion, not decided. ❓ = open question. Everything else is decided.*

---

## Theme
**Faith vs. love, and power vs. love.** The god thinks love is a weakness, or a tool for gaining power. It turns out to be the most powerful force there is, so valuable that others have been stealing it to manufacture mortals' love for the gods. In the end, he has to decide which the world needs more: **faith in the gods, or love between people.**

---

## The true story (in chronological order, secret)

1. **The Pantheon** is a realm of the gods where their **power over each other is limited or doesn't exist**.
2. **Faith and love are different kinds of power.** Mortals' love for each other is real but ordinary. **A god's love for a mortal, combined with faith, creates enormous celestial power.**
3. **The God of Time and the God of Memory** discovered this. They found a way to **harvest** that power and use it to **give mortals a feeling of love for the gods**. Without it, mortals would remember how hard life has been and how often their prayers went unanswered, and faith (and with it the Pantheon's power) would fade.
4. **The cycle (the loops):** the protagonist god leaves the Pantheon and falls in love with a mortal woman. Outside the Pantheon's protection, he's vulnerable. Time and Memory harvest the power of that love, then **wipe his memory and reset time**. The cycle starts again. **This has happened many times.**
5. To the protagonist, each reset looks like a **punishment** for loving a mortal. That's the cover story. In reality, the villains **need** him to fall in love with her again.
6. **The journal:** the god kept a journal of stories and memories about her in the Pantheon. Time's reset couldn't reach it, and **Time and Memory didn't know it existed**. It's written in **her people's language**.
7. **This loop is different:** thanks to the journal, the god **becomes aware early enough to stop them**.

## The order of reveals (what the player learns, and when)

| # | When | The player learns… | What the player believes now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start | I'm a god, I have no memory, these people are mine to guide | "I need to figure out who I am" |
| 2 | Act 1 | A strange journal of love stories about a mortal woman | "Some other god wrote this" |
| 3 | End of Act 1 | **My purpose is to bring this woman back**, but I don't know why | "She's important somehow" |
| 4 | Act 2 | Theories: a **sacrifice**, a **prophecy**, an **acolyte** who hears the gods | "She's the key to my power" *(wrong)* |
| 5 | ❓ Late Act 2 | **The journal is mine** | "I loved her once" |
| 6 | Act 3 | **Loving her made me weak.** I left the Pantheon for her, and that's how Time and Memory struck | "Love was my weakness, and it was worth it anyway" *(half-true)* |
| 7 | Act 3, final twist | **This isn't the first loop.** Time and Memory **need** our love. They've been **harvesting** it. I felt weak because they were draining it | "Love was never weakness. It was stolen." |
| 8 | Finale | Time and Memory reveal **what the power is for**: it makes mortals love the gods. Without it, faith fades and the Pantheon weakens. No one knows what comes after | "Is a world without faith but with love better or worse?" |
| 9 | The choice | **Break the cycle or keep it** → short epilogue → the game ends | — |

💡 **How Reveals 6 and 7 fit together:** Reveal 6 is what the god sincerely believes, and it's emotionally true: he chose love knowing it made him vulnerable. Reveal 7 adds that the weakness he felt **wasn't caused by love. It was the villains taking love's power.** The game first says "love is worth being weak for," then "love was never weak." Both beats land.

## Story logic: problems solved and open

### 1. ✅ If the villains *need* the love, why do they interfere?
**They're not fighting the love. They're fighting his awareness.** They want him to fall in love again *naively*.
- **Herding:** some of their "attacks" actually push him toward her. In Act 2 these look like hostility. After Reveal 7, the player realizes every attack moved them closer to her
- **Suppressing:** what they really fight is the **journal** and his **memory**: words going dark, rituals forgotten, signs of the past erased
- This gives the villain interference two layers, and a reason to replay the game

### 2. ✅ Why doesn't the journal exist in every loop?
**He's been writing it across many loops.** The Pantheon is out of Time's reach, so each time he falls in love, he adds pages, never remembering earlier ones. This loop, **there are finally enough pages** to wake him early.
- This explains why the pages are **out of order**: they come from different loops
- Some pages could be in **different inks or handwriting moods**. Some could describe meeting her "for the first time" more than once, which is a chilling clue that hints at Reveal 7 long before it happens
- The villains don't know about it because it never mattered before

### 3. ✅ What is the power for, and what happens if he defies them?

**What the power is for:**
- Time and Memory use the harvested divine-love power to **give mortals a feeling of love for their gods**: the protagonist, Time, Memory, and the whole Pantheon.
- Without it, mortals would remember their lives **as they actually were**: how hard life has been, and how long the gods sometimes went without answering their prayers. That real experience would **not** produce strong faith.
- Faith is what powers the gods. Without the manufactured love, faith weakens, and **the whole Pantheon weakens with it**.
- 💡 This is why it's *these two* gods: **Memory** shapes how mortals remember the gods (kindly, with the suffering softened). **Time** keeps resetting the cycle so the harvest never ends.

**The world if the cycle breaks:**
- A world **without faith, but with love**: mortals love *each other*, not the gods.
- The gods (the protagonist included) grow weaker, and maybe fade.
- **No one has ever broken the cycle**, so the outcome is **partly unknown**. That uncertainty is what Time and Memory warn him about.
- The protagonist (the player) decides whether that's a **better or worse future**. The game doesn't decide for them.

**The ending:**
- The final confrontation leads to a **choice**. Once the player makes it, there's a **short epilogue, and then the game ends**. We don't play out the consequences.

💡 **Ideas that build on this:**
- **The faith the player built was partly fake.** All game long, the player has been gathering faith as their power. The final twist reveals that some of it was **manufactured love**, harvested from the god's own love story. Breaking the cycle means **giving up the power the player spent the whole game building**. The gameplay resource *becomes* the sacrifice.
- **Silence has a cost.** Throughout the game, the player can choose to "remain silent" when people pray. In the ending, those unanswered prayers become the people's **real memories**. The player has been creating the very experience that would weaken faith without realizing it.
- **A personal epilogue.** The epilogue text can reflect **how the player actually treated their people**: how many prayers they answered or ignored, whether they ruled through love or fear. A world without manufactured love remembers the god *exactly as they were*. It's cheap to build (a few text variations) and very personal.
- **What does refusing to break the cycle look like?** ❓ Possibly: he lets Time and Memory continue, keeps the world's faith and the Pantheon strong, and accepts finding her and losing her again forever, maybe knowingly this time. Tragic, but defensible.

#### The final choice (draft)
| Choice | What happens | The epilogue shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Break the cycle** | The harvest ends. Mortals keep their love for each other but lose the manufactured love for the gods. Faith fades, and the Pantheon weakens | A world of people who love each other, remembering the gods honestly. He lives one life with her (❓ as a mortal?) |
| **Keep the cycle** ❓ | The harvest continues. Faith and the Pantheon stay strong | He forgets again, and the game ends on his awakening, but this time something is different (❓) |
| **The dark path** | (if the player chased power and fear all game) | The loop continues, and everything resets |

## The dark path

- The player **can** go down a darker path: treating her as a sacrifice or a tool, ruling through fear, chasing power
- The game **gently nudges them back toward the lighter path**. 💡 How:
  - **The villains herd the player:** they need the love ending, so dark choices meet more "bad luck" that pushes back toward her
  - **The journal stops making sense:** a culture built on fear develops different words, and her language stays unreadable
  - **Fear-faith doesn't combine with love:** faith built on fear gives quick power but can't become the special love + faith power
- 💡 **Dark ending: "the loop continues."** If the player commits fully to the dark path, Time and Memory reset everything again, and the game ends on the moment he wakes up, the same way the game began. This is a bad ending that fits the story, **without adding loops to the gameplay**.

## The journal (story side)
*(The gameplay rules for the journal are in `DESIGN.md`.)*

- It's a **love journal**, not instructions: stories and memories of their time together
- It's kept in the Pantheon, which is why it survived the reset
- It's **intact but unreadable**. It's written in **her people's language**, which doesn't exist yet after the reset. Words become readable as the civilization discovers the concepts behind them
- 💡 He wrote in her language because she taught it to him, or because the journal was written *to* her
- 💡 The god's own name is the last word to become readable

### Pages that read differently on a second look
Written to support the wrong theories in Act 2, these reveal their real meaning later:
- *"Without her, I am nothing."* → Act 2: she's my source of power. Act 3: love. After Reveal 7: literally true, since she's half of the power
- *"When she is near, I feel the whole Pantheon fall away."* → Act 2: transcendence. Act 3: he's losing his divine protection
- *"Her voice reaches me even here."* → Act 2: she's an acolyte who hears the gods. Act 3: he can't stop thinking about her
- *"I met her today. I've never felt anything like this."*, written in three different inks → only makes sense after Reveal 7
